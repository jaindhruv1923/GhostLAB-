Section 3 Indian Patents Act & FTO Advisory

1. Section 3(k) — Computer Programs & Algorithms

Under Section 3(k) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970, pure software algorithms and mathematical methods per se are non-patentable. However, when software produces a tangible technical effect on physical hardware (such as GhostLab's PID brushless motor control or PPG optical filters), it qualifies for statutory protection.

2. Section 53 — The 20-Year Public Domain Horizon

Section 53 mandates that every patent expires exactly 20 years from its international priority filing date. Once expired, all exclusive rights terminate irreversibly. GhostLab's Golden-Era Public Domain pool (e.g., US 6,213,219 rotary tillage fingers) is 100% free for Indian builders to manufacture and export worldwide without infringement liability.

3. The All-Elements Rule & Design-Around Jurisprudence

A claim is only infringed if the accused device embodies every element recited in the claim.
